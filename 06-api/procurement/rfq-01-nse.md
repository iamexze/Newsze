**Subject:** Market data licensing enquiry — subscription analytics platform, end-of-day and delayed

Dear NSE Data Licensing team,

I am writing to request licensing options and indicative pricing for NSE market data.

**What we are.** Newze is a pre-launch subscription platform publishing Indian market news
and analytics to retail and professional users over the web. We have no brokerage, no order
routing and no execution capability. We currently hold no market data licence and are
operating on synthetic data while we scope cost.

**What we need.** The full requirement is set out in the attached specification. In summary:
index levels, cash equity prices, futures and options including strike-level open interest,
participant-wise open interest, end-of-day files, the ban list and surveillance lists,
corporate actions, and point-in-time index constituent history — with roughly fifteen years
of history alongside the live feed.

**Three points that will shape your quote, and ours.**

**1. We want to launch on end-of-day data, not real time.** Our product is designed so that
latency is an attribute of each data field rather than a property of the whole system. We
would rather begin on end-of-day and add real time for paying subscribers later. Please quote
end-of-day, fifteen-minute delayed and real time as three separate line items.

**2. Much of what we display is computed, not quoted.** We derive and display open-interest
build-up classifications, put-call ratios, max pain, implied-volatility percentiles, index
contribution attribution and historical frequency statistics. We need to understand whether
such derived values are treated as licensed data, as derived data, or as our own work
product. This materially affects both cost and product design, and section 4 of the
specification sets out exactly what we compute.

**3. Historical data is our principal asset.** We store history permanently for statistical
analysis. Question 16 in the specification asks what happens to stored data on termination.
A straight answer to that question matters more to us than the headline fee.

We would also welcome guidance on whether a direct exchange agreement or an authorised
redistributor is the appropriate route for a company at our stage. We are approaching both.

I would be glad to arrange a call. We are collecting comparable proposals over the next three
weeks and would appreciate a response by `<date>`.
